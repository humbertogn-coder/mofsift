# Validation of the score components

**Question.** Does a component rank MOFs that have been synthesised above hypothetical ones?

**Data.** The QMOF database (`qmof.csv`, 20,372 structures, CC BY 4.0). It flags each structure
as synthesised (from the CSD and CoRE MOF) or hypothetical (five computer-generated sets) and
gives a MOFid decomposition into nodes, linkers and topology.

**Method** (`scripts/validate_score_components.py qmof.csv`; full output in
[`data/score_validation_qmof.json`](data/score_validation_qmof.json)):

- A MOF is counted once (same primary metal, linkers and topology): 10,595 distinct synthesised
  MOFs and 3,310 distinct hypothetical ones. Six hypothetical MOFs that coincide with a synthesised
  one were left out.
- The components are computed with the same code that scores candidates.
- Precedent components use 5-fold cross-validation: a synthesised MOF is never looked up in a
  reference that contains it.
- The number reported is the ROC-AUC: the probability that a synthesised MOF gets the better value
  than a hypothetical one. 0.5 means no separation.

**The caveat that matters.** The hypothetical MOFs are an *unlabelled* set, not a negative one.
Nobody has shown that they cannot be made, and some of the sets were built from well-known
building blocks. The AUC therefore measures how much a component makes a MOF resemble what has
already been made. It is not the accuracy of a synthesisability prediction.

## Result

| Component | Better when | ROC-AUC (mean ± s.d. over folds) | Separates? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `precedent_linker` | larger | 0.860 ± 0.003 | yes |
| `linker_size` | smaller | 0.799 ± 0.004 | yes |
| `linker_sc` | smaller | 0.743 ± 0.006 | moderately |
| `linker_sa` | smaller | 0.693 ± 0.007 | moderately |
| `precedent_pair` | larger | 0.676 ± 0.004 | moderately |
| `precedent_topology` | larger | 0.627 ± 0.015 | weakly |
| `linker_rotatable_bonds` | smaller | 0.623 ± 0.002 | weakly |
| `precedent_node` | larger | 0.446 ± 0.003 | **no** |

`precedent_topology` could be evaluated only for the MOFs with an assigned topology (3,910
synthesised, 3,222 hypothetical).

`precedent_node`, which is evaluated at the level of the metal element, does not separate the sets:
the hypothetical sets are built on the most common metals. It stays in the table as information
and is **not allowed in the aggregate**.

## It depends on the hypothetical set

AUC against each hypothetical set separately (sets with at least 10 distinct MOFs):

| Hypothetical set (distinct MOFs) | `linker_sa` | `linker_sc` | `linker_size` | `linker_rotatable_bonds` | `precedent_topology` | `precedent_node` | `precedent_linker` | `precedent_pair` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BoydWoo (1,802) | 0.788 | 0.817 | 0.837 | 0.681 | 0.587 | 0.307 | 0.872 | 0.681 |
| GMOF (1,294) | 0.563 | 0.646 | 0.752 | 0.543 | 0.671 | 0.635 | 0.853 | 0.677 |
| Haranczyk_MOF5 (79) | 0.408 | 0.493 | 0.528 | 0.534 | 0.419 | 0.095 | 0.715 | 0.559 |
| ToBaCCo (67) | 0.938 | 0.928 | 0.925 | 0.715 | 0.915 | 0.420 | 0.875 | 0.683 |
| Anderson (66) | 0.728 | 0.748 | 0.903 | 0.603 | 0.810 | 0.998 | 0.816 | 0.668 |

What this shows:

- The separation is largely a statement about how each hypothetical set was built. Sets assembled
  from large or elaborate linkers (ToBaCCo, BoydWoo) are easy to tell apart by linker size and
  complexity. The MOF-5 analogues, which put small functionalised terephthalates on one of the
  best-known nodes and nets, are not told apart by any linker score (AUC 0.41 to 0.53).
- No single component is reliable across the sets. `precedent_linker` is the most consistent
  (0.72 to 0.88).
- Values below 0.5 mean the hypothetical set looks *more* familiar than the average synthesised
  MOF on that component (for example the Zn4O node and the pcu net of the MOF-5 analogues).

## What has not been validated

- Whether the components predict the outcome of a synthesis. No dataset of failed syntheses was
  used; there is none of comparable size.
- The components on structures generated by mofsift itself. Their linkers are scored with the
  same code, but the benchmark's MOFs come from QMOF.
- The aggregate. It is a rank within one table; its usefulness depends on the weights chosen and
  has not been tested.
